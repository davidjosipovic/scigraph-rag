\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation over scholarly knowledge graphs is usually
evaluated with a large language model (LLM) acting as a judge. We build a
graded relevance gold set for 50 questions over the Open Research Knowledge
Graph (ORKG) and answer metrics that need no LLM judge, and use them to compare
two retrieval strategies inside one pipeline: graph-native SPARQL retrieval and
dense retrieval over text cards of all 68,806 ORKG papers. With context
construction, prompt and LLM held fixed, both reach similar retrieval quality
(normalized discounted cumulative gain at rank 8 of 0.72 and 0.73 on all
questions, 0.75 and 0.71 on the test split), but they retrieve largely
different papers, and a rank fusion of the two reaches 0.79. Graph retrieval is
better on method comparisons and claim verification, where it supplies the
deciding evidence; dense retrieval is better on topical questions, method usage
and title lookups. End-to-end answer quality is equal for two LLMs, and the
difference is smaller than the variance between repeated runs. Across nine
LLMs, an LLM judge correlates only moderately with the gold metrics (Spearman
correlation 0.45) and overstates the gap between cloud and local models.
\end{abstract}
