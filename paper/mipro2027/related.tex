\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Retrieval-augmented generation.} RAG grounds an LLM's answer in
retrieved documents \cite{lewis2020rag}; retrieval is usually dense, with
questions and documents embedded in one vector space \cite{karpukhin2020dpr}.
Open embedding models such as \texttt{mxbai-embed-large} \cite{mxbai2024} and
\texttt{nomic-embed-text} \cite{nussbaum2024nomic} make this possible
entirely on local hardware. Rankings from different retrievers are commonly
combined with reciprocal rank fusion \cite{cormack2009rrf}.

\textbf{Graph-based RAG.} GraphRAG approaches retrieve nodes, triples, paths
or subgraphs instead of text passages \cite{peng2024graphragsurvey}. A widely
used variant builds the graph from a text corpus with an LLM and summarises
its communities \cite{edge2024graphrag}. Han et al.\ \cite{han2025ragvsgraphrag}
compare such text-derived GraphRAG with standard RAG on question answering and
summarisation and propose hybrid strategies. In contrast, we retrieve from an
existing, curated knowledge graph, so no graph is built by an LLM, and we hold
everything except the choice of papers fixed.

\textbf{Question answering over ORKG.} The Open Research Knowledge Graph
describes papers by structured research contributions: problem, method,
data and results \cite{jaradeh2019orkg}. The SciQA benchmark poses questions
over ORKG that are answered by SPARQL queries \cite{auer2023sciqa}. Vanilla
LLMs fail on it, while fine-tuning and few-shot prompting with well-chosen
examples generate SPARQL much better \cite{lehmann2024sciqa}. Our setting
differs: questions are open (which papers compare two methods, does a claim
hold), and the answer is a text grounded in several papers' records rather
than one query result. Instead of generating SPARQL, the system plans
queries from templates per question type.

\textbf{Evaluating RAG.} RAG answers are commonly scored by an LLM judge
\cite{liu2023geval}. LLM judges can agree well with human preferences, but
show position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases \cite{zheng2023judging}.
RAGAS evaluates faithfulness without references by checking statements of
the answer against the retrieved context \cite{es2024ragas}, and FActScore
decomposes text into atomic facts verified against a knowledge source
\cite{min2023factscore}. We follow the claim decomposition but require a
verbatim quote and validate the judge. In addition, we score answers against a
graded relevance gold set, which needs no LLM judge, and use it to test how
well a typical judge tracks it.
